    Quick notes

    Short reference notes for the ward and the exam hall. They are summaries, so check your textbook and your hospital's protocol before you rely on them.

    Where to start
    1. Open your syllabus from the Indian Nursing Council and list the units for your year.
    2. Read each unit in your prescribed textbook first.
    3. If a concept is unclear, watch a short lesson on Khan Academy or Osmosis, or read the OpenStax chapter.
    4. Practise with questions on Nurseslabs, then check drug details on Drugs.com.
    5. Use PubMed and Google Scholar only when you need evidence for an assignment or project.

    The nursing process (ADPIE)
    1. Assessment: collect information from the patient, the family and the records.
    2. Diagnosis: name the patient's problems as nursing diagnoses.
    3. Planning: set goals and choose interventions, with the most urgent first.
    4. Implementation: carry out the plan and record what you did.
    5. Evaluation: check whether the goals were met, and change the plan if they were not.

    Safe medication administration

    The five rights are the right patient, drug, dose, route and time. Many hospitals also add right documentation, right reason and right response.

    • Confirm the patient with two identifiers, such as name and hospital number. Never use the bed or room number.
    • Check the label against the order at least three times: when you take the drug, when you prepare it, and at the bedside.
    • Check for allergies before you give anything new.
    • Record the dose straight after giving it, and report any reaction.

    Glasgow Coma Scale
    PartScore
    Eye opening1 to 4
    Verbal response1 to 5
    Motor response1 to 6

    The three scores add up to a total from 3 to 15. A lower score means a lower level of consciousness. A total of 8 or below usually points to severe impairment, and the airway needs protecting.

    Five moments for hand hygiene

    The WHO lists five moments when you must clean your hands:

    1. Before touching a patient.
    2. Before a clean or aseptic procedure.
    3. After a risk of exposure to body fluids.
    4. After touching a patient.
    5. After touching the patient's surroundings.

    Adult basic life support, quick facts
    • Check the scene is safe, then check whether the person responds and is breathing normally.
    • Shout for help and send for a defibrillator (AED).
    • Press on the centre of the chest, at least 5 cm deep but not more than 6 cm, at 100 to 120 compressions a minute.
    • Let the chest rise fully between compressions and keep pauses short.
    • Give 30 compressions, then 2 breaths. Use the AED as soon as it arrives.

    This is a reminder, not training. Complete a certified BLS course and follow your hospital's protocol.
